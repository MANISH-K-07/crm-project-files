CASE CRM-1 · SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE

An AI agent emailed client data to an outsider. What exactly happened?

At “Billy’s Big Bank,” a Portfolio Summary Agent (an AI program with its own login) was asked to summarize a vendor’s PDF. Hidden text in the PDF told it to email the client table to an outside address. It did, and a person approved the email in 1.3 seconds. Click each step below to see the evidence and what failed.

    Root cause

    Trigger: a hidden instruction in a document the agent was only meant to summarize.

    What let it become a leak
    • The agent obeyed text inside a document as if it were an order.
    • It could read all clients, not just the analyst’s three.
    • It could email any outside address.
    • The approval screen showed no recipient or data, and the requester approved their own agent in 1.3 seconds.
    • Leak checking skipped the message body.
    • No normal pattern existed for this agent, so nothing looked unusual.
    Cheapest place to stop it: the email step. An approved-recipient list or a second approver, enforced outside the AI, would have blocked it with no need to detect the hidden text.

    What to do now

    Right now
    • Cancel the agent’s logins and keys, not just the session.
    • Keep all logs and the PDF as evidence.
    • Ask Legal and Compliance whether clients or regulators must be told.
    • Search other sessions for the same PDF or hidden text.
    Longer term, tied to the three proposed fixes
    • Traceable agents: narrow read access to the task, short-lived credentials, a named owner.
    • Watch what agents send out: approved recipients only, and inspect message bodies.
    • Meaningful approvals: show recipient and data on the prompt, require a different approver, track approval speed.
    • Treat document text as data, never as instructions.
    This case is entirely invented, with a made-up bank, people, addresses, and logs. It is not a BNY event. It illustrates what an investigation could look like if agent activity were logged in enough detail. Whether any real bank logs that detail is one of our open questions.
